The saddle bag guide

Where the saddle bag came from, how to choose one and how to wear it for every occasion.

Origins

From tack room to wardrobe

For as long as people have ridden horses, they have needed somewhere to carry food, tools and documents. Leather saddlebags, often in pairs either side of the saddle, were the answer.

Designers borrowed that curved, sturdy shape for handbags. The fashion saddle bag became a hit in the late 1990s and has made several comebacks since, often in bold colours and prints.

Sizes

Choosing a size

Mini

Fits a phone, cards and keys. Perfect for evenings and festivals.

Small

Adds room for sunglasses and a small wallet. The most versatile everyday size.

Medium

Fits a compact umbrella or a small tablet. Good for travel days.

Quality checklist

What to look for

  • Even, tight stitching, especially where the strap meets the body.
  • Smooth, sealed edges with no fraying.
  • Hardware that feels heavy and opens easily.
  • A flap that lies flat and covers the opening fully.
  • A lining that is stitched neatly, with no loose threads.

Do

  • Adjust the strap so the bag sits where you can reach it.
  • Switch shoulders to spread the load.
  • Empty the bag regularly to keep its shape.

Don’t

  • Overfill a curved bag; it strains the flap and seams.
  • Hang a leather bag by its strap for months.
  • Leave it in a hot car or direct sun.
